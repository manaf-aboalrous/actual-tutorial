Reports: What Your Data Is Telling You

Reports turn your categorized transactions into honest insights about your financial life. After a month or two of real data, this becomes the most valuable part of Actual.

🎯 By the end of this lesson you will…
Know which reports and cards Actual provides
Know when to use Cash Flow, Net Worth, and Spending Analysis
Know what Crossover Point and Age of Money each tell you
Know when Calendar cards and Custom Reports are useful
Understand the experimental reports at a high level
Concept

The Reports dashboard: your financial mirror

The Reports section is separate from the budget page. Where the budget page tells you what you planned to spend, Reports tell you what actually happened. After even a month of use, the reports reveal things about your spending that will surprise you.

📍The Reports Dashboard - multiple widgets give you a complete financial overview. The dashboard is fully customizable; you can add, remove, and resize widgets.
💡
Besides the graphs and reports below, the dashboard can also hold Summary cards for a single quick number and Text widgets for your own notes and reminders.
Report 1

Cash Flow: "Did I save money this month?"

Cash Flow only looks at On Budget accounts, showing their balance over time along with separate income and expense visualizations. It gives you a picture of how income and spending affected your available money. It does not count transfers between your own accounts as income or expense, only real inflows and outflows.

📍Cash Flow - focuses on budgeted accounts, meaning money available for your budget.
How to use this report

In Tracking mode, compare cash flow against your Projected Savings. If Projected Savings said $550 but cash flow shows $200 growth, somewhere $350 went uncategorized or was undercounted. This nudges you to investigate.

Report 2

Net Worth: "Am I building wealth?"

Net worth is everything you own minus everything you owe - cash, investments, property equity, minus debts, mortgages, and loans. It's the single most important number in your financial life, and it should trend upward over time.

📍Net Worth over time - includes all accounts (On Budget and Off Budget). Add your mortgage and investment accounts as Off Budget accounts to see the full picture.
💡
Two views are available. Trend draws a single net worth line, which is the one to watch month to month. Stacked breaks the same total down by account, which is useful when you want to see which account is actually driving a change.
Report 3

Spending Analysis: expense trends over time

Spending Analysis tracks and compares your expenses over a selected period. Use it to see trends, fluctuations, and areas of high spending or savings - not just the single biggest category you spent on.

📍Spending Analysis - compare expenses across a selected period to spot trends, fluctuations, and areas of high spending or savings.
How to use this report for better budgeting

After 2–3 months, use this report to ask: which expenses are rising, fluctuating, or showing a savings opportunity? Use that insight to refine Tracking mode targets or adjust Envelope mode category funding.

Calendar card

Calendar card: income and expenses by day

The Calendar card displays daily income and expenses in a calendar format for a selected period. Use it to spot payday, rent, subscriptions, or any days that are unusually heavy on the budget.

📍Calendar card - a quick daily view of income and expenses.
Report 4

Crossover Point: "when could investment income cover my expenses?"

Crossover Point is a financial-planning report inspired by the book Your Money or Your Life. It estimates the month where passive income from your investments would be enough to cover your projected expenses, which is the point people usually mean by financial independence.

You configure it with the expense categories to count, the income or investment accounts to draw from, a safe withdrawal rate, and a method for projecting future expenses. Everything after that is a projection, so treat the date as a direction of travel rather than a promise.

📍The crossover is where the projected investment income line rises to meet projected expenses.
Report 5

Age of Money: "how long does money sit before I spend it?"

Age of Money measures the gap between the day money arrived in your budget and the day you spent it, averaged across your recent outflows. Transfers and income are excluded, so it reflects real spending only.

A rising number means you are increasingly spending money you earned a while ago, which is the practical definition of getting ahead of your bills. A number that keeps falling is an early warning that you are running closer to payday than you were.

📍The big number is the headline, with a trend arrow beside it showing which way it is moving.
💡
Thirty days or more is the usual benchmark. It means you are typically spending money you earned a full month ago rather than living paycheck to paycheck.
Action

Custom Reports: when the question is specific

Custom Reports let you analyze transactions as a table or chart: Bar, Line, Area, Donut, or Table. You can analyze Payments, Deposits, or Net, split results by category, group, payee, account, or month, and save the report back to the Reports page.

📍Custom report examples - bar, line, area, donut, and table views. Build once, save to the dashboard.
💡
Tip: Combine custom reports with tags for powerful specific analysis. Tag all holiday spending with #holiday2025, then build a custom report filtered to that tag - instant holiday budget summary across all categories and accounts.
Experimental

Experimental Reports: advanced reports for later

These reports are experimental, so they may have bugs, missing functionality, or change later. Do not start here in your first month; read about them when your data is more mature and you want deeper analysis.

Experimental

Sankey: where your money actually flows

A Sankey diagram draws money as streams whose thickness matches their size, so the big paths are obvious at a glance. It has two view modes. Spent follows income into your accounts and back out as spending. Budgeted shows the money available to budget and how it splits across categories.

📍Money flows left to right: from who paid you, into income, into your accounts, then out through category groups into individual categories. Thickness is proportional to the amount, so the widest ribbon is where most of your money went.
What to expect
  • A Sankey cannot draw negative numbers, so totals here can differ from the budget summary when you cover overspending from savings
  • Smaller flows collapse into an Other node to fit the space, and hovering it lists what is inside
  • It needs room, so use a desktop or tablet
Experimental

Budget Analysis: the budget page as a trend line

Where the Budget page shows one month at a time, this report plots it over time as four series: Budgeted, Spent, Overspending Adjustment, and a cumulative Balance. The numbers come straight from the Budget page, so it covers budget categories only, never transfers or Off Budget accounts.

📍Budgeted against Spent month by month, with the running Balance on top. A balance climbing steadily is the sinking-fund effect from Lesson 03 made visible.
⚠️
If a balance looks wrong, check rollover first. When a category has Rollover overspending enabled, its negative balance carries forward. When it is disabled, that negative is zeroed out and recorded separately in the Overspending Adjustment series. This is the single most common reason the numbers here surprise people.
Experimental

Balance Forecast: will I run short before payday?

This projects your balances forward so a shortfall shows up while you can still do something about it. It works out a starting balance from your posted history, then adds what it expects to happen next.

📍Posted history runs on into projected balances, and the report calls out the lowest point it finds. Here the line crosses zero in July and keeps falling, which is exactly the warning you want months ahead of time.
Source 1: your Schedules

The default. Actual expands each upcoming schedule into simulated occurrences up to the end date, applies the rules attached to them, and generates both legs of transfer schedules where it can. The running balance then updates day by day, or grouped by month if you prefer.

Source 2: your Tracking Budget

Available in Tracking Budget files. It starts from the current On Budget balance and walks forward month by month, adding budgeted income and subtracting budgeted expenses. It ignores schedules, account filters, and report filters, and is always monthly.

💡
The forecast is only as good as your schedules. If the bills you expect are not scheduled, the line will look healthier than reality. Planning further than twelve months ahead in Tracking Budget mode is not supported yet.
Experimental

Monte Carlo Analysis: will my savings outlast me?

Where Crossover Point asks when you could stop working, Monte Carlo asks what happens after you do. It replays your retirement thousands of times across different market outcomes and reports how often the money lasted, so the answer comes back as a success rate rather than a single prediction.

📍Configuration on top, results underneath. Here 5,000 simulated retirements running from age 55 to 99 succeeded 99.1% of the time, and the typical failure ran out at age 94.
💡
Treat it as a planning aid, not a forecast. Every number it produces rests on the assumptions you type in, and the historical returns behind it are US-only. The official documentation walks through each input if you want to set one up.
Experimental

Formula cards: build the number you actually want

When no built-in report answers your question, a Formula card lets you compute it. You define named queries, each holding its own filters and date range, then reference them in a spreadsheet-style formula: =QUERY("income") - QUERY("expenses") gives you a net income tile.

📍The card editor: the live result on top, the formula below it, and the Query Definitions panel on the right where you add the queries your formula can reference. Conditional colour sits at the bottom.
💡
Add one from Reports → Edit dashboard → Add widget → Formula card. This uses the same Excel formula mode toggle as the rule formulas in Lesson 07, so enabling it once covers both.
