Rules & Schedules: Let Actual Do the Work For You

Once your transactions are coming in and getting cleaned up correctly, use Rules and Schedules to reduce repetitive work. Rules clean and categorize imported transactions, while Schedules help you see recurring and expected transactions before they happen.

🎯 By the end of this lesson you will…
Understand what rules are and how they work
Know how Actual creates rules automatically from your behaviour
Know how to view, edit, and create rules manually
Understand rule stages and how Schedules support automation
Know when a rule formula is better than a fixed value
Be able to create a schedule and know how it matches a real transaction
Concept

What a rule is: an "if → then" instruction

Every time a transaction is imported, Actual runs it through all your rules in order. A rule has two parts: conditions (when to fire) and actions (what to change). If all conditions match, all actions are applied.

The most common use is cleaning up ugly payee names and setting categories automatically. For example:

If all these conditions are true…
Imported payeecontains"amazon"
Then apply these actions…
Set Payee→Amazon
Set Category→Shopping

This turns AMAZON.COM*5C7QC7MH0 AM 10/26 into "Amazon / Shopping" every single time - automatically.

📍The same rule in the real editor. The stage selector sits at the top, the conditions come next, and the actions follow underneath.
The Good News

Actual writes most rules for you automatically

You don't need to build your rule library from scratch. Actual watches your behaviour and creates rules based on what you do:

A
When you rename a payee
Actual asks: "Apply this rename automatically in future?" Click yes and it creates a pre-stage rule. That ugly bank name will never appear again.
B
When you categorize a transaction
After you assign the same payee to a category a couple of times, Actual creates a rule to apply that category automatically going forward. After a few weeks of use, most transactions categorize themselves on import.
✨
The payoff: After a month or two of regular use, importing a week's worth of transactions takes about 2 minutes - Actual cleans up the names, assigns categories, and you just review and confirm. This is what makes the system sustainable long-term.

Conditions can check: imported payee, payee, account, category, date, notes, amount (inflow or outflow), and cleared status. All string matching is case-insensitive.

Actions can set: payee, category, notes, account, date, amount, cleared status. They can also append or prepend text to notes.

Practical

Rule stages: controlling the order

Rules run in three stages. Knowing this prevents confusion when multiple rules affect the same transaction.

pre
Pre-stage runs first, always. Actual automatically puts payee-renaming rules here. This ensures payee names are cleaned up before any categorization rules run.
default
Default-stage is where most rules live. Within this stage, more specific rules (using is) automatically rank after broader ones (using contains), so the specific rule always wins.
post
Post-stage runs last, always. Use this for rules that must override everything else. Most users never need this.
📍Stage of rule sits at the top of every rule. This one runs at Post so it marks anything from the Venmo or Cash accounts as cleared after all other rules have had their turn.
Action

Viewing and managing your rules

1
Go to More → Rules in the sidebar
See all your rules, including the ones Actual created automatically for you.
2
Click "edit" on any rule to view it in detail
The editor shows a live list of existing transactions that match your conditions - excellent feedback before saving.
3
Select transactions and click "Apply actions" to backfill
Retroactively apply a rule to past transactions - great for re-categorizing a batch of old records in one click.
💡
The rule editor is also a powerful bulk editor. Create a temporary rule with conditions that match what you want to change, apply actions to selected transactions, then delete the rule. Much faster than editing transactions one by one.
New & Experimental

Rule formulas: a calculated value instead of a fixed one

Everything above sets a value you typed in advance. Sometimes the right value depends on the transaction itself. Rule formulas let an action calculate what to set, using spreadsheet-style expressions.

Turn it on at Settings → Show advanced settings → Experimental features → Excel formula mode. In the rule editor, a ƒ button appears on Set actions. Click it and that action switches to formula mode. Every formula starts with =.

What a formula can read
  • amount and balance, both in cents, where a positive amount means income
  • date and today, in YYYY-MM-DD form
  • notes, imported_payee, payee_name, account_name, category_name
  • cleared and reconciled, as true or false
Two examples to start from
  • Label the direction of money: =IF(amount<0, "Expense", "Income")
  • Pull in another account's running balance: =BALANCE_OF("Savings")
  • Show cents as a normal amount by dividing: =amount / 100
💡
Formula rules can also set split amounts, which is the quickest way to peel a fixed tax or fee off every transaction from one payee.
⚠️
Limits worth knowing. A rule formula is a single line; line breaks are collapsed into spaces. It cannot set the payee, category, or account, because those fields need internal IDs rather than text. Formulas and rule templating are mutually exclusive on the same action, and templating is now the legacy option: formulas replaced it.
Planning

Schedules: recurring and expected transactions

Schedules help you see upcoming transactions before they happen: salary, rent, subscriptions, loan payments, or credit card autopay. They can be recurring or one-time, and can be entered automatically or left for manual approval.

📍Schedules overview - recurring or expected transactions appear here so you can see what is coming before it happens.
Use them for
  • Recurring income like salary
  • Bills, subscriptions, and loan payments
  • Credit card autopay
  • Approximate variable bills like utilities
Why they help
  • Show upcoming items in the account before payment date
  • Reduce manual entry for recurring transactions
  • Can be linked with Rules for categories and notes
  • Help you avoid surprise bills
💡
Start with only three or four schedules: salary, rent, key subscriptions, and card autopay if you use it. Add more when you notice a recurring pattern.
Action

Creating a schedule: the fields that matter

The schedule dialog looks busy, but only a few fields change how it behaves. The rest is naming and bookkeeping.

📍A monthly internet bill. Repeats turns the date into a frequency, the next three occurrences are previewed underneath, and existing transactions that match are offered for linking when you save.
Date and Repeats

Leave Repeats off for a one-time expected transaction. Turn it on and the Date field becomes a frequency: monthly, every two months, every two years, or specific days you pick with the + button. That last option handles things like three phone lines billed on different days under a single schedule.

Amount

The dropdown next to the amount offers is exactly, is approximately, or a range between two figures. Approximate matches anything within 7.5% either way, so a 100 bill matches between 92.50 and 107.50. That is usually enough for utilities, and the Schedules list marks these with a tilde.

💡
If a due date lands on a weekend, Move schedule shifts it to the working day before or after. Schedules do not know about public holidays, so those still need a manual nudge.
💡
Shortcut from a transaction you already typed. Enter a transaction dated in the future and Actual offers a Schedule button on it. That opens this same dialog with the date, payee, and amount already filled in, which is the quickest way to turn a bill you just recorded into a repeating schedule.
⚠️
Automatically add transaction is the one decision to think about. Leave it off and the schedule shows up as an upcoming reminder that you approve when the real transaction arrives. Turn it on and Actual posts the transaction on every scheduled date whether or not the money actually moved. Auto-add suits fixed, reliable payments like a mortgage. For anything variable, manual approval keeps your balance honest.
Three ways in

You rarely need to build one from scratch

A
Let Actual find them for you
Click "Find schedules" at the bottom of the Schedules page and Actual scans your history for anything that looks recurring, then offers to create each one. This is the fastest way to set up after a few months of data.
B
Build one from a transaction you already have
Tick a transaction in the account, then choose "Link schedule" from the menu at the top right and pick "Create New". Every field arrives prefilled from that transaction, so you only adjust the frequency.
C
Add a new schedule by hand
Use "Add new schedule" on the Schedules page for anything that has not happened yet, such as a subscription starting next month.
📍Found Schedules suggests recurring patterns and shows the frequency it detected. If something you expected is missing, the payee names probably differ across those transactions, which is exactly what the payee cleanup in Lesson 06 fixes.
Concept

How a schedule finds its real transaction

A schedule is a prediction until a real transaction matches it. The rule to remember is the date: it has to fall within two days either side of the scheduled date. Keeping the payee name consistent matters too, since a schedule is tied to its payee.

This trips people up when a biller takes the money early. If your power bill is scheduled for the 15th but the company collects on the 10th, the posted transaction sits outside the window and the schedule keeps showing as upcoming, as though the bill were still to come.

The cleanest fix is to link them yourself: select the posted transaction, choose Link schedule from the menu at the top right or press S, then pick the schedule it belongs to. If you would rather not, select the upcoming entry and choose Skip scheduled date, or leave it and let the next occurrence match normally.

📍Scheduled items sit at the top of the account register in italics, tagged Upcoming or Due, above your real transactions. This is the payoff: you can see what is coming before it lands.
💡
How far ahead they appear is the upcoming length, set from the Schedules page and overridable per schedule. An annual insurance renewal can show a month out while everyday bills stay at a week. This changes what you see, not what is budgeted.
🔗
Schedules and Rules meet here. Open a schedule and click Edit as rule to see the rule behind it, already carrying the date, frequency, and account as conditions. Add a category and a note there and every future entry from that schedule arrives fully filled in.
🔗
Schedules and Budget Automation work together. The "Cover schedule" automation from Lesson 03 budgets straight from a schedule, either saving up for the next occurrence or covering it in the month it lands. Automations reference schedules by ID, so renaming a schedule no longer breaks the link.
